RECOMMENDATION

60-second answer

What should the team remember before committing to a CTRI wrapper?

Do not build a production CTRI scraper or a patient matcher. Validate a narrower service: source-linked general trial discovery, separately time-stamped site verification, and a human-owned inquiry/referral task. First obtain written data-access and reuse terms.
FACT

Programmatic access

CTRI has public HTML records, but its current search surfaces require CAPTCHA and CSRF protection. No documented public CTRI API, bulk machine export, open-data licence, or developer terms were found.

OPEN

Permission

Public visibility does not settle automated access, caching, derivative fields, redistribution, or commercial use. Written CTRI/rights-holder terms are a product gate.

FACT

Alternative feeds

WHO ICTRP offers CSV/XML and a partner web service, but its terms materially restrict commercial use and storage. ClinicalTrials.gov offers API v2, but does not replace CTRI coverage.

BOUNDARY

Clinical scope

General search and operational verification are lower-risk. Patient-note ingestion, eligibility scoring, trial ranking, or treatment-oriented claims cross into clinical decision support and possible SaMD review.

Condition that changes the recommendation: if target users only act on patient-specific eligibility rankings, the current safe concept has no product-market fit; do not smuggle matching into the wrapper.

RUNTIME VIEW

Critical success and failure flow

How should a source record become a visible operational assertion without pretending to be live?

Success: exact source fact plus recent human site assertion. Failure: no rights, invalid schema, unresolved identity, source outage, or expired verification remains visible and never becomes “current.”

Failure contract

  • Source unavailable: serve last snapshot only with stale banner and retrieval date.
  • Schema changed: quarantine affected records; do not publish partial reinterpretation.
  • Uncertain duplicate: preserve both records and queue human review.
  • No site response: display “no authoritative response,” never “closed.”

Correction contract

  • Never overwrite the original registry value.
  • Store normalized value and rule/version separately.
  • Store site assertion with source, actor, time, and expiry.
  • Send source corrections only through an authorised human workflow.
STATE VIEW

Three lifecycles, not one status

Which state belongs to the registry, the verifier, and the referral task?

Invariant: a registry state never automatically becomes a site assertion, and a site assertion never automatically becomes an eligibility decision.

Forbidden states: eligible, best_match, recommended, or an inferred clinical reason. The trial team owns formal eligibility.
RISK ASSESSMENT

Regulatory and policy boundary

Is there a rule that prevents doctors from using a wrapper?

OBSERVED No reviewed Indian rule or CTRI page categorically prohibits a doctor from using a wrapper that displays public general trial information. The consequential questions concern the product operator and intended use—not the doctor's ability to open a website.

CTRI

Registry inclusion is not approval or endorsement. Information is registrant-supplied. A wrapper must not imply CTRI/ICMR validation.

CDSCO

Standalone software meeting the notified medical-device definition is regulated. Intended use and error consequences drive classification; CDSCO is the competent classifier.

ICMR / Ethics Committee

Recruitment advertisements, notices, methods, payments, privacy, and undue inducement belong in ethics review. Registry text is not blanket permission to solicit patients.

Point of risk: freeze the wording of product claims before regulatory classification. “Search public trials” and “recommend the best trial for this patient” are different products.

This artifact is an evidence-backed risk map, not legal or regulatory advice. Written CTRI data terms and qualified Indian review remain required before a production or commercial commitment.

DECISION GATES

Questions to answer before idea or architecture lock

Which answers can invalidate the idea or materially change the architecture?

Gate A — Problem evidence
  1. Which exact role owns the job: treating oncologist, research coordinator, tumour-board coordinator, site PI, or research office?
  2. What event triggers a search, and how many times did it occur in the last four weeks?
  3. What happened in the last five de-identified searches: sources, elapsed time, calls, failure, workaround, outcome?
  4. Is the dominant failure discovery, stale contact/status, screening, eligibility, travel/affordability, or referral follow-through?
  5. Would a role, SOP, or shared directory solve it more safely and cheaply than software?
Gate B — Data authority and rights
  1. Which source owns each field: CTRI, ClinicalTrials.gov, sponsor, or local site?
  2. Is written permission available for automated access, caching, derivatives, redistribution, and commercial use?
  3. Which fields are essential, and can names, personal contacts, and uploaded documents be omitted?
  4. What are refresh cadence, lag, outage behavior, schema-change notice, and correction route?
  5. How are duplicate registrations linked, and who reviews uncertain matches?
Gate C — Safe intended use
  1. What exact words appear in pitch, interface, label, and instructions: search, verified contact, likely eligible, or recommended?
  2. Does any output depend on diagnosis, stage, biomarker, treatment history, laboratory result, or performance status?
  3. Could sorting, badges, or alerts reasonably be interpreted as eligibility or treatment advice?
  4. Will the product send recruitment material, and who approves the exact content?
  5. What written legal, Ethics Committee, hospital, and CDSCO classification reviews are required?
Gate D — Operating model and incentives
  1. Who verifies sites, through which authorised channel, how often, and with what evidence?
  2. What happens when registry, sponsor, and site disagree—or nobody responds?
  3. Who pays: hospital, network, sponsor, site, research programme, or another party?
  4. Why will sites maintain status: fewer unsuitable calls, better referrals, portfolio visibility, or a measured benefit?
  5. What is fully loaded verification cost per active trial/site and monthly support burden?
Gate E — Lifecycle, reliability, and governance
  1. What states and transitions are allowed for source record, verification assertion, and task?
  2. When does verification expire, and how is history retained without appearing current?
  3. What happens on source outage, schema change, record disappearance, invalid data, or unresolved conflict?
  4. Which actions require human approval, role-based access, immutable audit, and institutional review?
  5. What data are retained, where, for how long, and how are correction and deletion handled?
Gate F — Pilot and sign-off
  1. Which bounded indication, geography, site set, and users create a valid 60–90-day test?
  2. What are the primary KPI, guardrails, and explicit stop thresholds?
  3. What comparator is credible: baseline workflow, SOP, shared spreadsheet, or tool?
  4. What result proves aggregation is insufficient but verification plus handoff is valuable?
  5. Who has authority to accept evidence and lock product scope?
Architecture gate: do not select framework, database, deployment topology, or integrations until the permitted source, intended-use claim, patient-data boundary, verification owner, buyer, record volume, refresh cadence, and audit requirement are named.
PROVISIONAL LOGICAL MODEL

Responsibilities if the gates pass

What is the simplest logical decomposition without prematurely selecting a stack?

Source adapter

Uses only authorised access. Captures source ID, raw value, retrieval time, version, and access result. Stops on rights or schema failure.

Provenance store

Retains immutable source snapshots and normalized values separately. The official registry remains system of record for registry facts.

Normalization reviewer

Runs deterministic mappings. Queues uncertain identities, duplicates, and conflicts for a human; never invents missing facts.

General search

Indexes disease, geography, phase, declared status, provenance, and verified operational state—never patient features.

Verification workbench

Lets an authorised steward record site assertion, method, evidence, time, discrepancy, and expiry.

Task and audit ledger

Records human approval, owner, sent/acknowledged/closed status, and immutable events without deciding eligibility.

CHEAPEST USEFUL EXPERIMENT

Validate the service before the software

What experiment reduces product, data-access, and operating-model uncertainty at the same time?

  1. Choose one target role, one centre/network, and one bounded cancer/region query set.
  2. Manually assemble a small source-linked record set; do not scrape or store patient data.
  3. Ask the authorised site contact to verify the operational state and preferred referral route.
  4. Give target users a simple searchable view and human-owned inquiry log.
  5. Compare against the current workflow and a shared-sheet/SOP alternative.
  6. Stop unless both burden and maintainable verification value are observed.
TimeMedian time to authoritative site answer
FreshnessShare verified within approved expiry window
ConflictRegistry-versus-site discrepancy rate
HandoffInquiry acknowledgement and human closure

Falsifiers

  • Searches are too rare or fast to create material burden.
  • Stale status/contact is not a meaningful cause of failed referrals.
  • The dominant constraint is trial scarcity, eligibility, cost, or travel rather than information.
  • A role, SOP, or shared sheet performs as well with lower operating cost.
  • Authorised data access cannot support the planned use.
  • Site verification cannot be kept current affordably.
  • Users require patient-specific ranking, which is outside scope.

MENTAL MODEL

Recall and review

Can the team explain the decision without reopening every source?

Registry fact
A value submitted to and displayed by a source registry. It remains attributed to that source.
Normalized value
A deterministic derivative for search. It never replaces the original value and carries a rule version.
Site assertion
A time-bounded operational statement from an authorised named human/site source.
Freshness
Time and evidence context—not a claim that a record is clinically correct or a site has capacity.
General discovery
Search by trial attributes without using an individual's clinical facts.
Eligibility
A protocol-based patient-specific determination owned by the trial team and outside the current product.

  3. Source record, site verification assertion, and human inquiry/referral task.
