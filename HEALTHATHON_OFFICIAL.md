## Programme facts

- Health-a-thon 2026 describes itself as India’s first Doctor × Technology health hackathon for assistive AI.
- Clinical domains: Cancer Care, Diabetes Care, and Maternal & Child Health.
- The selected project stream is **Doctor / Care Team Facing**.
- Doctor/care-team theme: simplify workflows, reduce administrative load, and support care coordination.
- Patient/caregiver theme: guide, engage, and empower patients and caregivers through the care journey.
- The programme focuses on non-clinical, operational, and workflow problems: assistive, not diagnostic.
- Teams contain 2–5 people and require one practising-doctor partner and one technical lead.
- The site states a ₹54+ lakh prize pool split evenly across the three clinical tracks. The Top 30 teams reach the finale, and every finalist is awarded.
- Grand finale: 28 November 2026 at IIT Bombay, Powai, Mumbai.

## Hard scope boundary

### In scope

- Healthcare workflows, operations, and continuity of care.
- Patient engagement and administrative processes.
- Solutions for doctors, care teams, patients, and caregivers.
- Workflow-first tools with light implementation and integration effort.
- Existing AI capabilities, agents, orchestration frameworks, APIs, and open-source tools.

### Out of scope

- Diagnosis.
- Treatment recommendations.
- Clinical decision support.
- Clinical risk scoring.
- Interpretation of medical data.
- Autonomous clinical advice.

### Safety and delivery expectations

- Humans remain in control.
- Every automated or AI-assisted step permits human override.
- Important actions are auditable.
- The product must be usable in a busy clinic with low training and a clear next step.
- Multilingual and caregiver-friendly behavior matters where the users require it.
- Strong entries target a frequent workflow problem, name a measurable operational KPI, and show a credible path to pilot evidence in 60–90 days.
- Phase-one integration should be light and should meet clinics where they are: paper, spreadsheets, PDFs, scans, images, WhatsApp, or hospital systems.
- Only fake or fully anonymised data may be used. No patient data may be placed on the programme platform, shared with mentors, or used in demos.

## Doctor / care-team-facing use cases

### 01. Patient Follow-up & Continuity of Care

**Summary:** Keep patients connected across months of care and surface who needs follow-up before they are lost to care.

**Current reality:** Long-term care spans months or years. Patients miss appointments, investigations, treatment milestones, and reviews. Relevant information is split across appointment systems, EMRs, laboratory records, treatment registers, spreadsheets, and manual trackers. Care teams spend substantial time finding patients who need attention, prioritising interventions, and coordinating departments. Manual processes become unsustainable as volume grows, producing delayed interventions and continuity gaps.

**Desired direction:** Help teams identify follow-up needs early, improve visibility across fragmented information, coordinate follow-up across roles, simplify existing workflows, and support timely patient-centred follow-up at scale.

### 02. Consultation Readiness & Patient Journey Review

**Summary:** Start each consultation with a complete longitudinal view rather than reconstructing it across systems.

**Current reality:** Preparing for follow-up requires reconstructing a clinical journey from notes, laboratory investigations, imaging reports, treatment records, medication history, and pending actions. Each consultation builds on earlier decisions, so clinicians need to know what changed, what remains pending, and what needs attention. Information may be spread across systems or exist as scanned reports, PDFs, images, and patient-provided documents. Busy outpatient clinics leave little time for assembly, increasing administrative work and reducing patient interaction.

**Desired direction:** Bring together relevant information from diverse sources into a complete, contextual, longitudinal view. Help clinicians find meaningful changes, pending investigations, milestones, medication history, and consultation context. Support structured and unstructured inputs including EMRs, labs, imaging reports, prescriptions, discharge summaries, referral letters, scans, PDFs, photographs, and patient-provided records. Identify missing or inconsistent information and allow clinicians to gather more context where required.

### 03. Patient Registry & Population Health

**Summary:** Give care teams a live population view of gaps, priorities, and longitudinal journeys.

**Current reality:** Teams must monitor people across the full care continuum, not only during clinic visits. They need to identify patients needing attention and ensure investigations, treatments, screenings, and follow-up milestones occur on time. The data is spread across EMRs, disease registries, laboratories, spreadsheets, and programme databases. Manual longitudinal tracking becomes difficult as volume grows.

**Desired direction:** Maintain a comprehensive, longitudinal, real-time panel view; consolidate multiple systems; produce patient- and population-level operational insights; identify care gaps and patients needing attention; support registries and coordinated proactive care.

### 04. Clinic Operations & Patient Flow

**Summary:** Run busy OPDs with real-time flow, smarter slots, and fewer bottlenecks.

**Current reality:** Clinics coordinate appointments, arrivals, no-shows, walk-ins, delayed investigations, cancellations, and changing priorities across doctors, nurses, front-desk teams, and coordinators. Capacity and demand require continuous adjustment. Inefficiency causes waiting, unused capacity, bottlenecks, and missed opportunities for timely care.

**Desired direction:** Proactively manage appointments, queues, schedules, vacant slots, clinically urgent cases, and communications. Provide real-time operational visibility and recommendations without disrupting existing workflows.

### 05. Doctor Productivity & Knowledge Assistant

**Summary:** Provide an assistive knowledge and content co-pilot for teaching, research, and administration while the clinician controls the output.

**Current reality:** Doctors create educational material, presentations, manuscripts, referrals, patient information, reports, protocols, and other content while reviewing literature and guidelines. These repetitive, context-dependent activities compete with care, research, education, and innovation.

**Desired direction:** Help clinicians create, review, refine, summarise, adapt, and organise professional content using available context, institutional knowledge, literature, and preferences. Support knowledge discovery, evidence synthesis, education, research, and routine professional tasks.

### 06. Patient Education & Digital Engagement

**Summary:** Create accurate multilingual education and campaigns without making clinicians full-time content teams.

**Current reality:** Long-term care requires ongoing education about awareness, lifestyle, adherence, preventive care, milestones, self-management, and misinformation. Clinicians increasingly create handouts, campaigns, social content, video scripts, presentations, newsletters, blogs, patient stories, and multilingual messages. Maintaining accuracy and consistency across channels creates operational burden.

**Desired direction:** Create, adapt, manage, and deliver context-aware educational material across online and offline channels; support communication calendars and personalised education journeys; keep clinicians in control of accuracy and final messaging.

### 07. Clinic Performance & Practice Growth

**Summary:** Improve referrals, retention, reputation, and practice insight without added administrative work.

**Current reality:** Doctors manage professional visibility, trust, referral networks, community engagement, digital presence, and operational efficiency. Information about acquisition, retention, service use, feedback, referrals, and online activity is fragmented.

**Desired direction:** Support visibility, engagement, referral relationships, online presence, reputation, and data-driven practice growth. Provide operational insight into acquisition, retention, utilisation, communication, and performance.

## Patient / caregiver-facing use cases

### 01. Care Journey Companion

Stage-aware guidance for patients and caregivers: what comes next, how to prepare, what actions are required, and when to seek appropriate medical advice. It should adapt to the individual’s condition, stage, and evolving needs while complementing rather than replacing the patient-provider relationship.

### 02. Long-term Care Engagement

Sustain participation over weeks, months, or years across medicines, investigations, appointments, lifestyle changes, self-monitoring, milestones, and warning signs. Adapt engagement to condition, stage, progress, and circumstances, and strengthen connections among patients, caregivers, and care teams.

### 03. Family & Caregiver Support

Give caregivers consent-respecting visibility into care plans, appointments, medicines, milestones, and care requirements. Improve education, tasks, alerts, coordination, and shared planning without bypassing patient privacy or existing clinical workflows.

### 04. Healthcare Navigation & Access

Provide multilingual, accessible, context-aware guidance through registration, appointments, investigations, consultations, hospital processes, pharmacies, billing, referrals, and follow-up, especially for elderly, rural, chronically ill, and low-health-literacy users.

### 05. Financial & Administrative Support

Help patients understand and navigate insurance benefits, reimbursement, government schemes, assistance programmes, eligibility, documents, applications, status, and multilingual administrative guidance.

### 06. Patient Health Journey Progress

Present a clear longitudinal view of completed milestones, ongoing treatments, investigations, preventive care, upcoming work, and future milestones so patients and caregivers can understand progress and prepare for the next stage.

## Round 1 submission content

One shared team entry addresses five areas:

1. The exact problem and what changes if it is solved.
2. What the team will build and the technology approach.
3. Why this team is suited to build it.
4. Whether any part already exists.
5. An end-to-end deck.

A prototype link, video walkthrough, and supporting document are optional. The track and solution focus come from the doctor partner’s registration; the team may adjust the entry’s use-case wording without overwriting the doctor’s original registration.

## Official FAQ answers relevant to product direction

### How are doctors paired with technologists?

Doctors name a workflow problem and choose track/use cases. Technologists list skills and ideas. Doctors review recommendations and choose; the platform does not assign a team. Existing complete teams skip matchmaking.

### Is coding required for doctors?

No. The doctor defines the problem and guides clinical fit; technologists build. Each team needs a doctor partner and technical lead.

### What may teams build?

Assistive operational workflow tools such as follow-up and recall, notes and documentation, patient navigation, and workflow automation. Nothing may diagnose, prescribe, replace clinical judgement, or act as clinical decision support. Human override and audit trails are expected for operational actions. Only fake or fully anonymised data may be used.

### What makes a strong entry?

A frequent workflow pain; usability for doctors and patients; near-term impact on low-cost channels; light first-phase integration; a measurable operational KPI; multilingual and caregiver support where needed; human override and auditability; pilot evidence within 60–90 days; and compatibility with information already present in paper registers, Excel, PDFs, scans, WhatsApp, or hospital systems.

### Is a measurable KPI required?

Yes for a strong entry. Examples on the site include recall completion, time-to-note, no-show rate, and handoff lag. The team should state how it will measure baseline and change.

### How much integration is expected?

Minimal in phase one. Do not propose a full HIS/EHR rewrite. Meet the clinic’s current information environment.

### Must the product be multilingual?

Where the users need it, yes. Do not assume English if the care setting is not English-only. Treat caregivers as first-class users where family support is part of the workflow.

### What does evidence in 60–90 days mean?

A credible real-care-setting pilot that moves an operational KPI, even at small scale. A stage demo or one-off prototype is insufficient.
