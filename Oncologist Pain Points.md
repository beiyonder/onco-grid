### **The 5 core problems**

I would reduce these into five overarching pain points:

**1\. FIND**

> *“Finding the exact information I need takes too long.”*

Guidelines, dosing, toxicity, staging, trials, publications and drug information are fragmented.

**2\. CONTEXTUALIZE**

> *“I need information relevant to this specific patient and clinical situation.”*

Cancer \+ stage \+ biomarker \+ line of therapy \+ previous treatment \+ comorbidities.

**3\. SYNTHESIZE**

> *“The patient's information is scattered across multiple reports; I need one coherent view.”*

Reports → pathology → imaging → molecular information → treatment history → concise summary.

**4\. ACT**

> *“I need practical, trusted information at the point of care.”*

Dose modifications, toxicity grading, drug interactions, approved indications, trial availability and guideline comparison.

**5\. CONNECT**

> *“Sometimes I need to discuss a case or question with another expert.”*

Peer discussion, knowledge sharing, research and presentation preparation.

### **Most important insight from the doctors**

**Doctor X** also cautioned that experienced oncologists may not want an AI tool making treatment recommendations because they already follow established international protocols and prefer authentic sources. The perceived value was stronger around **knowledge sharing, clearing doubts, clinical discussions and academic work**.

So the underlying opportunity:

> **Not “AI that tells oncologists what treatment to give.”**  
> **Rather, “a trusted oncology workspace that helps oncologists find, synthesize, contextualize and use the right information faster.”**
