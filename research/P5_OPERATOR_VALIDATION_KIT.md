# P5 — Process-only operator validation kit

## Purpose

Use this kit to validate one narrow tumour-board workflow without asking for an oncologist, patient record, clinical recommendation, screenshot, or filled case.

The question is operational:

> After clinicians author the final tumour-board decision, who records it, makes it available to the authorised treating unit, confirms receipt, owns explicitly assigned non-clinical coordination, and keeps completion or unresolved status visible?

This is not a treatment-adherence study and not a request to judge whether a board recommendation was clinically correct.

No outreach has been sent. This file prepares a safe, repeatable request.

## Why one more operator account is necessary

Public evidence has narrowed but not closed the gap:

- The 2026 NCRP hospital survey reports heterogeneous documentation and communication and says 48.2% of reported boards lacked a follow-up system (`EV-0019`).
- NCG/KCDO already specifies a human-entered final decision and a later review of whether the first decision was followed (`EV-0014`).
- NCG and iECHO already support substantial pre-board submission, review, correction, notification, and sharing (`EV-0036`–`EV-0040`).
- A draft NABH oncology HIS/EMR annexure already expects selection, board IDs, scheduling, notifications, integrated review, attendance, and documentation of multidisciplinary inputs and follow-ups (`EV-0042`).
- One Eastern India hospital used preformed Excel lists, entered the board decision after the meeting, and used a patient care coordinator to collect treatment and follow-up data (`EV-0043`).

These sources show that a broad tumour-board product would duplicate existing capability. They do not establish whether acknowledgement and operational disposition are absent or burdensome in a current target setting.

## Evidence priority for the next participant

Seek one person who performs or directly supervises the workflow. Priority order:

1. tumour-board secretariat or coordinator;
2. patient care coordinator attached to oncology;
3. hospital-based cancer registry principal investigator or registry operator;
4. designated nodal person responsible for tumour-board operations;
5. oncology programme administrator;
6. medical-records or quality officer who audits tumour-board documentation;
7. presenting clinical team member only if no operational role exists.

Do not assume these titles are interchangeable. Record the local title and actual work.

## Current public routing path

The recommended first route is the official [NCG Virtual Tumor Board support and inquiry page](https://www.ncgindia.org/key-initiatives/virtual-tumor-board). It currently describes recurring host-centre sessions and publishes a VTB support contact.

Ask that contact to route the process-only request to the person who handles post-session records or programme operations. Do not assume the public support contact personally owns the handoff.

Alternative institutional routes are the programme offices for the currently listed host centres, Tata Memorial Hospital and Max Hospital Saket. Request an operations, secretariat, registry, records, or quality role—not a clinical opinion.

Do not commit a person's email address or phone number. Resolve the current contact from the live official page immediately before outreach. Sending requires owner confirmation for the exact recipient, channel, and final message.

## Participation screen

Continue only if the person can describe at least one of these without patient details:

- where the final human decision is recorded;
- how the authorised treating unit receives it;
- whether receipt or review is acknowledged;
- how non-clinical coordination items are assigned;
- how pending, completed, deferred, rejected, or unresolved states are tracked;
- how aggregate tumour-board activity is counted.

Do not treat general familiarity with tumour boards as workflow evidence.

## Initial outreach draft

Sending this message requires owner approval for the exact recipient and channel.

> Hello. We are studying the operational workflow around oncology tumour boards—not the clinical decision itself.
>
> We are looking for a 20-minute process-only walkthrough from someone who coordinates, documents, audits, or follows tumour-board work. We do not need patient names, records, recommendations, screenshots, or any confidential information.
>
> We want to understand what happens after clinicians author the final decision: where it is recorded, how the treating unit receives it, whether receipt is acknowledged, who owns any explicitly assigned coordination step, and how completion or unresolved status is tracked.
>
> Aggregate counts and a completely blank template would be useful if your institution permits them. We will not attribute the workflow to you or your institution without explicit permission.
>
> Would you be the right person for this process discussion? If not, which operational role usually handles it?

Do not send a product pitch or claim that the hospital has a workflow gap.

## Twenty-minute process walkthrough

### Minute 0–3 — Establish the local board

1. What is your role in relation to the tumour board?
2. What work do you personally perform before, during, or after it?
3. How often does the board meet?
4. Roughly how many cases are discussed in a normal meeting or month?
5. Is there a designated secretariat, coordinator, nodal person, or patient care coordinator?

Record estimates as estimates. Do not request a case list.

### Minute 3–8 — Start at the human decision

> Please start at the moment clinicians finish the discussion and author the final decision. What happens next?

Probe one step at a time:

1. Who enters or finalises the decision?
2. In which artifact and system?
3. Is it signed, approved, or locked? By whom?
4. Is the board record separate from the ordinary clinical record?
5. If the records differ, which one is authoritative?
6. Who needs to receive or review the decision?
7. How is it made available—EMR, paper, written summary, message, email, direct conversation, or another route?
8. Does anyone record that the treating unit received or reviewed it?

Do not ask what treatment was recommended.

### Minute 8–13 — Ownership and status

1. Can the board decision contain explicitly assigned next steps?
2. Which of those are operational rather than clinical?
3. Who assigns an owner?
4. Where is the owner recorded?
5. Are target dates recorded?
6. Which statuses are used?
7. Who checks pending items?
8. What happens when an item is unresolved?
9. Is it escalated before the next board or only reviewed later?
10. Does anyone enter the same information twice?

Examples of potentially operational work include making an authorised record available, booking an appointment already ordered, obtaining an existing document, routing an authorised referral, or scheduling a later review. The participant—not the researcher—must classify the local step.

### Minute 13–17 — Burden and failure

1. In a normal week or month, how many discussed cases enter this workflow?
2. What percentage has the decision recorded in the normal clinical system?
3. What percentage reaches the intended treating unit through the expected route?
4. Is acknowledgement measured today?
5. Roughly how many coordinator or staff minutes are spent per case?
6. Which step creates the most calls, messages, searches, or duplicate entry?
7. How often is ownership unclear?
8. How often does an item remain unresolved without a visible status?
9. What existing tool comes closest to solving this?
10. Would another layer reduce work or create more duplication?

“Not measured” is a valid result. Never pressure the participant to invent a number.

### Minute 17–20 — Falsify and close

1. Is there actually a material problem here?
2. Does your existing EMR, register, spreadsheet, or coordinator process already handle it reliably?
3. Is the phrase “follow-up system” mainly about clinical outcomes rather than operational handoff?
4. Which assumption in our workflow map is wrong?
5. Is the real burden somewhere else—before the board, in patient navigation, or in another process?
6. May we send you a de-identified process map for correction?
7. Is there a completely blank, institution-approved template or field list we may review?

Close by repeating that no patient or clinical decision information should be sent.

## Blank-artifact request

Request only a blank, publicly shareable, or institution-approved artifact:

- tumour-board submission form;
- agenda or case-list column headings;
- minutes template;
- final-decision template;
- communication or acknowledgement field list;
- action/follow-up tracker headings;
- aggregate audit-report field list;
- written SOP describing roles and handoffs.

Ask the owner to remove:

- real names and signatures;
- patient or staff identifiers;
- contact details;
- sample clinical content;
- institutional credentials;
- access links or internal paths;
- hidden spreadsheet rows, comments, metadata, and revision history.

Do not accept a filled example and attempt to de-identify it later.

## Scope classification worksheet

For every requested field or action, choose one:

| Class | Meaning | Product treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Operational metadata | Actor, timestamp, authorised recipient, acknowledgement, owner, due date, status, audit event | Potentially allowed |
| Human-authored clinical reference | Link or immutable reference to an existing clinician-authored decision | Potentially allowed with access control; do not rewrite |
| Clinical interpretation | Meaning derived from diagnosis, stage, pathology, imaging, response, toxicity, prognosis, or risk | Exclude |
| Clinical decision support | Patient-specific ranking, alert, eligibility, urgency, or recommended next clinical action | Exclude |
| Treatment recommendation | Treatment choice, dose, sequence, continuation, or modification | Exclude |
| Patient advice | Autonomous patient-specific medical guidance | Exclude |

If a status requires deciding whether treatment was clinically correct, it is not an operational status for this project.

## Candidate success criteria

Candidate B becomes eligible for owner product-lock review only if one current workflow account establishes all of the following:

1. a named operational role owns or should own the handoff;
2. one exact step is materially incomplete, delayed, duplicated, or invisible;
3. the step occurs often enough to measure;
4. an aggregate denominator and baseline exist or can be collected safely;
5. the output can reference a human decision without interpreting it;
6. the first integration can use an existing export, reference, or approved template;
7. the workflow removes work rather than adding a second source of truth;
8. existing NCG, NABH-aligned, iECHO, and local systems do not already solve it adequately;
9. institutional access, retention, acknowledgement, and audit rules can be stated;
10. the strongest contradiction remains visible in the problem contract.

## Fallback rule

Return Candidate B to discovery or reject it if:

- the local EMR already records and routes the signed decision reliably;
- the treating unit already acknowledges receipt through the normal workflow;
- a coordinator already tracks ownership and completion without material burden;
- “follow-up system” refers only to clinical-outcome research;
- no role owns the handoff and there is no operational demand for one;
- the proposed layer requires duplicate entry;
- the useful status depends on clinical interpretation;
- the institution cannot permit the minimum information flow;
- no measurable baseline exists and no safe measurement plan is feasible.

The first fallback is Candidate A, source-linked outside-record consultation readiness, but it must pass its own actor and person-time evidence gate.

## Evidence handling

- Store only de-identified process notes, aggregate counts, and approved blank artifacts.
- Do not put personal contact details in the repository, ledger, Serena, Mnemopi, prompts, or reports.
- Record a public or approved source locator; otherwise describe the evidence as a private operator account without publishing identifying information.
- Separate direct statements from interpretation.
- Add a ledger record only after provenance, consent boundary, method, limitations, and scope class are complete.
- If identifying information appears, stop, exclude it from notes, and follow the agreed deletion process.
